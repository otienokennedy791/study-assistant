1. Introduction to Curriculum

A curriculum is a planned set of learning experiences designed to achieve specified educational goals and objectives.

In nursing education, the curriculum guides what students should learn, how they should learn it and how learning will be evaluated.

2. Types of Curriculum
  • Formal curriculum
  • Informal curriculum
  • Hidden curriculum
  • Null curriculum
  • Core curriculum
  • Integrated curriculum

The different forms describe different aspects of what learners are intentionally or unintentionally taught.

3. Principles of Curriculum Development

Curriculum development should be systematic and responsive to educational, professional and societal needs.

  • Relevance
  • Flexibility
  • Continuity
  • Integration
  • Balance
  • Sequence
  • Participation
4. Needs Assessment

Needs assessment identifies gaps between the current situation and the desired knowledge, skills, attitudes or competencies of learners or professionals.

Information may be obtained from learners, educators, employers, professional bodies, communities and research.

5. Curriculum Aims, Goals and Objectives

Aims are broad statements describing the overall educational purpose.

Goals describe broad outcomes expected from a program or course.

Objectives are specific statements of what learners should be able to demonstrate.

6. Bloom's Taxonomy

Bloom's taxonomy organizes learning objectives according to levels of cognitive complexity.

Commonly taught cognitive levels include:

  1. Remember
  2. Understand
  3. Apply
  4. Analyze
  5. Evaluate
  6. Create
7. Curriculum Content Selection

Content should be selected according to learner needs, professional requirements, available evidence and educational goals.

  • Relevance
  • Accuracy
  • Significance
  • Appropriateness
  • Current evidence
8. Curriculum Organization

Curriculum organization determines how content and learning experiences are arranged.

Important principles include sequence, continuity, integration and balance.

9. Teaching-Learning Strategies

Teaching strategies are methods used by educators to facilitate learning.

  • Lecture
  • Discussion
  • Demonstration
  • Simulation
  • Case study
  • Problem-based learning
  • Clinical teaching
10. Clinical Education

Clinical education provides opportunities for nursing students to apply theoretical knowledge and develop clinical skills in real or simulated healthcare environments.

Clinical learning should include appropriate supervision, feedback, assessment and patient safety measures.

11. Curriculum Implementation

Curriculum implementation is the process of putting a planned curriculum into practice.

Important requirements include appropriate teachers, learning resources, facilities, leadership and student support.

12. Curriculum Evaluation

Curriculum evaluation determines whether a curriculum is achieving its intended educational outcomes.

  • Student performance
  • Graduate competence
  • Teaching effectiveness
  • Learning resources
  • Program outcomes
13. Curriculum Models

Curriculum models provide frameworks for planning and organizing curriculum development.

Examples include the Tyler model, Taba model and competency-based approaches.

14. Competency-Based Education

Competency-based education focuses on learners demonstrating specified knowledge, skills and attitudes required for effective professional practice.

Assessment is based on demonstrated competence rather than time alone.

15. Curriculum Review and Revision

Curricula should be periodically reviewed to ensure they remain relevant to changes in healthcare, technology, professional practice and society.

Feedback from students, educators, graduates, employers and professional bodies can contribute to curriculum revision.

The Tyler approach is commonly summarized around four questions:

  1. What educational purposes should be achieved?
  2. What learning experiences can achieve them?
  3. How can these experiences be effectively organized?
  4. How can we determine whether the purposes are being achieved?

Long-Answer Questions

1. Discuss the curriculum development process.

Curriculum development is a systematic process of designing, implementing and evaluating educational programs.

The process begins with a needs assessment to identify gaps between existing and desired knowledge, skills, attitudes and competencies.

The next step is establishing broad aims and goals, followed by the development of specific and measurable learning objectives.

Appropriate content is then selected and organized according to principles such as relevance, sequence, continuity and integration.

Appropriate learning experiences and teaching strategies are selected. The curriculum is then implemented using suitable educators, facilities, learning resources and student support systems.

Finally, the curriculum is evaluated to determine whether the intended outcomes have been achieved. Findings from evaluation can be used to revise and improve the curriculum.

2. Explain the importance of curriculum evaluation.

Curriculum evaluation determines whether the planned curriculum is achieving its intended educational outcomes.

  • It identifies strengths and weaknesses of the curriculum.
  • It helps determine whether learning objectives are being achieved.
  • It provides information for improving teaching and learning.
  • It helps identify gaps in content or learning experiences.
  • It supports curriculum revision.
  • It helps determine whether graduates have the competencies required for professional practice.

3. Discuss Bloom's revised taxonomy and its application in nursing education.

Bloom's revised taxonomy is a framework for classifying cognitive learning objectives according to increasing levels of complexity.

The levels are:

  1. Remember: recalling information.
  2. Understand: explaining or interpreting information.
  3. Apply: using knowledge in a situation.
  4. Analyze: breaking information into parts and examining relationships.
  5. Evaluate: making judgments using criteria.
  6. Create: producing something new from existing knowledge.

In nursing education, the taxonomy helps educators develop objectives, teaching activities and assessment methods that move learners from basic recall toward clinical reasoning and higher-order thinking.

4. Discuss factors that should be considered when developing a nursing curriculum.

Curriculum development should consider the needs of learners, the nursing profession, healthcare systems and society.

  • Learner needs: existing knowledge, abilities and learning needs.
  • Healthcare needs: current and emerging health problems.
  • Professional requirements: standards and competencies required for nursing practice.
  • Technological changes: new healthcare technologies and learning technologies.
  • Available resources: educators, facilities, equipment and finances.
  • Societal needs: cultural, community and social expectations.
  • Regulatory requirements: applicable educational and professional standards.
