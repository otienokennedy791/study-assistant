1. Introduction to Oncology

Oncology is the branch of medicine and nursing concerned with the prevention, diagnosis, treatment and supportive care of people with cancer.

  • Cancer is characterized by uncontrolled abnormal cell growth.
  • Malignant cells may invade surrounding tissues.
  • Cancer cells can spread to distant organs through metastasis.
  • Oncology nurses provide physical, psychological, educational and supportive care.
  • Important nursing goals include symptom control, treatment support, patient education and quality of life.
2. Cancer Biology and Cell Growth
  • Normal cells grow, divide and die in a controlled manner.
  • Cancer develops when mechanisms controlling cell growth become abnormal.
  • Mutations may affect genes responsible for cell growth and repair.
  • Malignant cells may demonstrate uncontrolled proliferation.
  • Angiogenesis allows tumors to develop their own blood supply.
  • Metastasis occurs when malignant cells spread from the primary site to other parts of the body.
3. Causes and Risk Factors of Cancer

Risk factors may be modifiable or non-modifiable.

  • Tobacco exposure
  • Alcohol consumption
  • Some infections
  • Radiation exposure
  • Occupational and environmental exposures
  • Obesity and physical inactivity
  • Family history and genetic susceptibility
  • Increasing age

Nurses play an important role in health education and prevention.

4. Cancer Prevention and Early Detection
  • Primary prevention aims to reduce cancer risk before disease develops.
  • Secondary prevention focuses on early detection.
  • Healthy lifestyle practices can reduce the risk of some cancers.
  • Vaccination can prevent certain infection-associated cancers.
  • Patients should be encouraged to participate in appropriate screening programmes.
  • Early recognition of warning signs can support timely assessment.
5. Cancer Screening

Screening aims to identify disease or precancerous changes before symptoms become apparent.

  • Breast cancer screening may involve mammography according to applicable guidelines.
  • Cervical cancer screening may involve HPV testing, cytology or other approved methods.
  • Colorectal cancer screening may involve stool tests or endoscopic examination depending on guidelines.
  • Screening recommendations depend on age, risk factors and national guidelines.
6. Cancer Diagnosis and Staging
  • History and physical examination are important initial steps.
  • Laboratory investigations may support diagnosis and treatment planning.
  • Imaging may help identify tumors and possible spread.
  • Biopsy provides tissue for pathological examination.
  • Staging describes the extent of cancer in the body.
  • The TNM system commonly describes tumor, regional lymph nodes and metastasis.
  • Accurate staging helps guide treatment planning and prognosis.
7. Principles of Cancer Treatment
  • Surgery
  • Chemotherapy
  • Radiotherapy
  • Hormonal therapy
  • Targeted therapy
  • Immunotherapy
  • Supportive and palliative care

Treatment may be used with curative, disease-control or palliative intent depending on the cancer and clinical situation.

8. Chemotherapy

Chemotherapy uses medicines that affect cancer cells. Some medicines also affect rapidly dividing normal cells.

Common adverse effects

  • Nausea and vomiting
  • Fatigue
  • Alopecia
  • Myelosuppression
  • Mucositis
  • Changes in appetite

Nursing care: monitor blood counts, infection signs, bleeding, hydration, nutritional status and treatment-related symptoms.

9. Radiotherapy

Radiotherapy uses ionizing radiation to damage cancer cells.

  • It may be external beam or internal radiation therapy.
  • Fatigue is a common effect.
  • Skin reactions may occur in the treated area.
  • Mucositis may occur when certain areas are irradiated.
  • Patients should follow instructions from the radiation oncology team regarding skin and treatment-site care.
10. Surgery in Cancer Management
  • Diagnostic surgery may obtain tissue for diagnosis.
  • Curative surgery aims to remove the cancer.
  • Debulking may reduce tumor size when complete removal is not possible.
  • Palliative surgery may relieve symptoms.
  • Nursing care includes preoperative preparation and postoperative monitoring.
  • Important postoperative concerns include pain, infection, bleeding, nutrition and wound care.
11. Breast Cancer
  • Breast cancer is a common malignancy affecting breast tissue.
  • Possible warning signs include a breast lump, changes in breast shape or skin, nipple changes or abnormal discharge.
  • Diagnosis may involve clinical assessment, imaging and biopsy.
  • Treatment depends on cancer type and stage.
  • Possible treatments include surgery, systemic therapy and radiotherapy.
  • Nurses provide education, emotional support and postoperative care.
12. Cervical Cancer
  • Persistent infection with high-risk HPV types is a major cause of cervical cancer.
  • Prevention includes HPV vaccination and appropriate screening.
  • Possible symptoms include abnormal vaginal bleeding or discharge, particularly in more advanced disease.
  • Treatment depends on stage and may include surgery, radiotherapy and systemic treatment.
  • Nurses have an important role in screening education and treatment support.
13. Prostate Cancer
  • Prostate cancer develops in prostate tissue.
  • Risk increases with age.
  • Assessment may include history, examination, PSA testing and other investigations.
  • Some patients may have urinary symptoms while others may have no symptoms early in the disease.
  • Treatment depends on risk category, stage and patient factors.
14. Lung and Colorectal Cancer

Lung cancer

  • Tobacco exposure is a major risk factor.
  • Possible symptoms include persistent cough, chest discomfort, breathlessness and unexplained weight loss.

Colorectal cancer

  • Possible symptoms include changes in bowel habits, blood in stool, abdominal discomfort and unexplained weight loss.
  • Screening can help identify colorectal cancer or precancerous lesions.
  • Treatment may involve surgery and systemic therapy, with radiotherapy used in selected situations.
15. Palliative and End-of-Life Care
  • Palliative care focuses on quality of life and relief of suffering.
  • It can be provided alongside disease-directed treatment.
  • Important symptoms include pain, nausea, breathlessness, fatigue and psychological distress.
  • Nurses provide symptom assessment, communication, emotional support and family education.
  • End-of-life care should respect patient dignity, preferences and applicable ethical and legal requirements.

Cancer Warning Signs
  • Unexplained weight loss
  • Persistent or unexplained pain
  • Unusual bleeding or discharge
  • Persistent cough or hoarseness
  • Changes in bowel or bladder habits
  • Changes in a mole or skin lesion
  • A persistent lump or thickening
  • Difficulty swallowing

These signs do not automatically mean cancer, but persistent or unexplained changes require appropriate medical assessment.

Chemotherapy Nursing Care
  • Check treatment orders and patient identification.
  • Monitor vital signs and relevant laboratory results.
  • Assess for nausea, vomiting and dehydration.
  • Monitor for infection and bleeding when myelosuppression is possible.
  • Assess the oral cavity for mucositis.
  • Provide nutritional support and patient education.
  • Observe for treatment-specific adverse reactions.
  • Follow institutional hazardous-drug handling procedures.
Palliative Care
  • Focus on comfort and quality of life.
  • Assess symptoms regularly.
  • Manage pain according to the prescribed plan.
  • Address psychological, social and spiritual needs according to patient preferences.
  • Communicate clearly with patients and families.
  • Support advance care planning where appropriate.
  • Respect dignity and patient autonomy.

Short-Answer Questions

1. Define cancer.
Cancer is a group of diseases characterized by abnormal cells that grow and divide uncontrollably and may invade surrounding tissues or spread to distant sites.
2. State five risk factors for cancer.
Examples include tobacco exposure, alcohol consumption, certain infections, radiation exposure, obesity, physical inactivity, increasing age and genetic susceptibility.
3. What is metastasis?
Metastasis is the spread of malignant cancer cells from the primary tumor to distant tissues or organs.
4. State four common adverse effects of chemotherapy.
Common effects include nausea and vomiting, fatigue, alopecia, myelosuppression and mucositis.
5. What is palliative care?
Palliative care is an approach that focuses on improving quality of life and relieving suffering associated with serious illness through management of physical, psychological, social and other needs.
6. State four roles of an oncology nurse.
Roles include patient assessment, treatment and medication support, symptom management, patient education, psychological support, coordination of care and monitoring for complications.
7. Differentiate between benign and malignant tumors.
Benign tumors generally do not invade surrounding tissues or metastasize, whereas malignant tumors can invade surrounding tissues and may spread to distant sites.
8. State four methods of cancer treatment.
Examples include surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, hormonal therapy, targeted therapy and immunotherapy.

Long-Answer Questions

These are useful for examination practice. Click to reveal a model answer.

1. Discuss the nursing management of a patient receiving chemotherapy.

Nursing management begins with assessment of the patient's diagnosis, treatment plan, allergies, baseline observations and relevant laboratory results.

The nurse should monitor for common adverse effects such as nausea and vomiting, fatigue, mucositis, alopecia and myelosuppression. When myelosuppression occurs, the patient may be at increased risk of infection or bleeding.

The nurse provides prescribed antiemetics and other supportive medicines, encourages appropriate nutrition and hydration, assesses the oral cavity, monitors elimination and provides psychological support.

Patient education is essential. The patient should understand the treatment schedule, possible adverse effects, warning signs requiring urgent review and the importance of attending follow-up appointments.

2. Discuss the role of the nurse in cancer prevention and early detection.

The nurse contributes to primary prevention through health education about tobacco avoidance, healthy nutrition, physical activity, appropriate alcohol use and other modifiable risk factors.

Nurses also educate communities about vaccination and prevention of infection-associated cancers where applicable.

In secondary prevention, nurses encourage eligible individuals to participate in appropriate cancer screening programmes and help patients understand the purpose of screening.

Nurses should also teach people about warning signs and encourage timely medical assessment of persistent or unexplained symptoms.

3. Discuss the nursing management of a patient receiving radiotherapy.

The nurse assesses the patient's baseline condition and explains the treatment process and expected effects.

During treatment, the nurse monitors for fatigue, skin reactions and other site-specific effects such as mucositis when relevant.

The nurse provides education about prescribed skin care and advises the patient to follow the radiation oncology team's instructions regarding the treatment area.

Emotional support, nutritional assessment, symptom management and communication with the multidisciplinary team are important components of care.

4. Discuss the principles of palliative and end-of-life nursing care.

Palliative nursing focuses on comfort, quality of life and relief of suffering.

The nurse regularly assesses symptoms such as pain, nausea, vomiting, breathlessness, fatigue and psychological distress.

Care should address physical, psychological, social and other needs according to the patient's preferences and circumstances.

Effective communication with the patient and family is essential. The nurse supports dignity, autonomy, appropriate decision-making and culturally respectful care.
