About ENT Nursing

Ear, Nose and Throat nursing focuses on the assessment, prevention, treatment and nursing management of disorders affecting the ear, nose, sinuses, throat, larynx and related structures.

The ENT nurse should understand anatomy and physiology, assessment techniques, common ENT disorders, hearing and communication problems, perioperative care, emergencies and patient education.

📖 Study Notes

External Ear

The external ear collects sound waves and directs them toward the tympanic membrane.

  • Pinna: Collects and funnels sound waves.
  • External auditory canal: Conducts sound toward the tympanic membrane.
  • Tympanic membrane: Vibrates in response to sound waves.
📖 Study Notes

Middle Ear

The middle ear contains the auditory ossicles and helps transmit and amplify vibrations.

  • Malleus
  • Incus
  • Stapes
  • Eustachian tube
📖 Study Notes

Inner Ear

  • Cochlea: Important for hearing.
  • Vestibule: Contributes to balance.
  • Semicircular canals: Detect rotational movement.
  • Auditory nerve: Carries auditory information toward the brain.

📖 Study Notes

Otitis Externa

Otitis externa is inflammation or infection of the external auditory canal.

Manifestations
  • Ear pain
  • Itching
  • Ear discharge
  • Tenderness of the external ear
  • Possible hearing reduction
📖 Study Notes

Otitis Media

Otitis media is inflammation or infection involving the middle ear.

Possible manifestations
  • Ear pain
  • Fever
  • Hearing impairment
  • Irritability in children
  • Possible tympanic membrane changes
📖 Study Notes

Ménière's Disease

Ménière's disease is associated with episodes of vertigo, fluctuating hearing loss, tinnitus and a sensation of fullness in the affected ear.

📖 Study Notes

Types of Hearing Loss

  • Conductive hearing loss: Problem with transmission of sound through the external or middle ear.
  • Sensorineural hearing loss: Involves the inner ear, auditory nerve or central auditory pathways.
  • Mixed hearing loss: Contains both conductive and sensorineural components.
📖 Study Notes

Nursing Communication Strategies

  • Face the patient when speaking.
  • Speak clearly at an appropriate pace.
  • Reduce background noise.
  • Use written communication when necessary.
  • Confirm understanding.
  • Ensure hearing aids are functioning when used.

📖 Study Notes

Rhinitis

Rhinitis is inflammation of the nasal mucosa. It may be allergic, infectious or associated with other irritants.

  • Nasal congestion
  • Runny nose
  • Sneezing
  • Itching
📖 Study Notes

Sinusitis

Sinusitis involves inflammation of the paranasal sinus mucosa and may cause facial pressure, nasal obstruction and nasal discharge.

Nursing care
  • Assess pain and nasal symptoms.
  • Encourage prescribed treatment.
  • Promote adequate fluid intake when appropriate.
  • Provide education regarding medication adherence.
📖 Study Notes

Epistaxis

Epistaxis refers to bleeding from the nose.

Basic first-aid principles
  • Sit the patient upright and lean slightly forward.
  • Encourage gentle pressure on the soft part of the nose.
  • Monitor the patient.
  • Seek medical attention when bleeding is severe, persistent or associated with significant trauma.
📖 Study Notes

Tonsillitis

Tonsillitis is inflammation of the tonsils, commonly associated with infection.

  • Sore throat
  • Difficulty swallowing
  • Fever
  • Enlarged or inflamed tonsils
  • Possible cervical lymphadenopathy
📖 Study Notes

Laryngitis

Laryngitis is inflammation of the larynx and commonly presents with hoarseness or altered voice.

Nursing advice
  • Encourage appropriate voice rest.
  • Promote hydration when appropriate.
  • Address underlying causes.
  • Monitor breathing when symptoms are severe.

Tracheostomy

A tracheostomy is a surgically created opening through the neck into the trachea to establish an airway.

📖 Study Notes

Routine Nursing Care

  • Assess airway patency.
  • Monitor respiratory status.
  • Maintain appropriate humidification.
  • Provide stoma and tube care according to policy.
  • Manage secretions appropriately.
  • Ensure emergency airway equipment is available.
  • Communicate using methods appropriate to the patient's condition.
📖 Study Notes

Potential Complications

  • Airway obstruction
  • Bleeding
  • Infection
  • Tube displacement
  • Subcutaneous emphysema

1. Discuss the nursing management of a patient with otitis media.

Nursing management begins with assessment of the patient's symptoms, including ear pain, fever, hearing changes and any discharge. The nurse should also assess the patient's general condition and relevant history.

Prescribed medications should be administered correctly and the patient should be monitored for response to treatment and possible complications.

Pain management is important. The nurse should provide appropriate prescribed analgesia and comfort measures.

Patient education should include medication adherence, follow-up care and instructions to report worsening symptoms, persistent fever, severe pain or deterioration in hearing.

2. Discuss the nursing care of a patient with hearing loss.

The nurse first determines the degree and type of hearing impairment and assesses how it affects communication and daily activities.

Communication should be adapted to the patient's needs. The nurse should face the patient, speak clearly, reduce background noise and use written communication when necessary.

If the patient uses a hearing aid, the nurse should ensure that it is available, functioning and appropriately maintained.

Emotional support is also important because hearing impairment may affect social interaction, independence and confidence.

3. Discuss the nursing management of a patient with a tracheostomy.

The primary nursing priority is maintaining airway patency and adequate ventilation. Respiratory rate, oxygenation, breath sounds and the patient's overall respiratory condition should be monitored.

The tracheostomy tube and stoma should receive appropriate care according to institutional policy. Secretions should be managed using appropriate techniques.

The nurse should remain alert for complications such as tube obstruction, displacement, bleeding and infection. Emergency airway equipment should be readily available.

The patient and family should receive education about tracheostomy care, communication, warning signs and follow-up.
