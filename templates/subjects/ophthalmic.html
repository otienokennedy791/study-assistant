About Ophthalmic Nursing

Ophthalmic nursing focuses on the assessment, prevention, treatment and nursing management of patients with disorders affecting the eye and vision. The nurse requires knowledge of eye anatomy and physiology, assessment techniques, common ophthalmic diseases, surgical care, medication administration and patient education.

Study tip: Study each topic through its smaller subtopics. Pay particular attention to anatomy diagrams, assessment procedures, manifestations, treatment, nursing management and complications.
📖 Study Notes

External Structures of the Eye

The external structures protect the eye and help maintain its normal function.

  • Eyelids: Protect the eye from injury and excessive light.
  • Eyelashes: Help prevent dust and foreign materials from entering the eye.
  • Conjunctiva: Thin mucous membrane covering the inner eyelids and anterior sclera.
  • Lacrimal apparatus: Produces, distributes and drains tears.
  • Extraocular muscles: Control movement of the eyeball.
📖 Study Notes

Internal Structures

  • Cornea: Transparent anterior structure that provides much of the eye's refractive power.
  • Sclera: Tough white outer layer providing protection and shape.
  • Iris: Colored part of the eye that regulates the amount of light entering.
  • Pupil: Opening through which light enters the eye.
  • Lens: Transparent structure that changes shape during accommodation.
  • Retina: Contains photoreceptors that convert light into neural signals.
  • Optic nerve: Carries visual information to the brain.
📖 Study Notes

Functions of Major Structures

  • Cornea – refraction and protection.
  • Iris – controls pupil size.
  • Lens – fine focusing of light.
  • Retina – receives light stimuli.
  • Optic nerve – transmits visual impulses.
  • Extraocular muscles – coordinate eye movement.
📖 Study Notes

Visual Pathway

Light is focused onto the retina where photoreceptors convert the stimulus into electrical signals. These signals travel through the optic nerve, optic chiasm and optic tract before reaching visual areas of the brain.

📖 Study Notes

History Taking

The nurse should obtain information about the patient's visual symptoms, onset, duration and factors that worsen or relieve them.

  • Blurred vision
  • Eye pain
  • Redness
  • Photophobia
  • Eye discharge
  • Loss of vision
  • Trauma or foreign body exposure
  • Previous eye surgery
  • Use of spectacles or contact lenses
  • History of diabetes or hypertension
📖 Study Notes

Visual Acuity

Visual acuity measures the ability to distinguish details at a specified distance. It is commonly assessed using a Snellen chart.

The nurse should ensure the patient is positioned correctly, test one eye at a time and record the result accurately.

📖 Study Notes

Pupillary Assessment

  • Assess pupil size.
  • Assess pupil shape.
  • Assess equality.
  • Assess direct response to light.
  • Assess consensual response.
  • Assess accommodation where appropriate.

📖 Study Notes

Definition

A cataract is an opacity of the normally transparent lens of the eye that interferes with the passage of light and may result in progressive visual impairment.

📖 Study Notes

Causes and Risk Factors

  • Increasing age
  • Diabetes mellitus
  • Eye trauma
  • Prolonged corticosteroid exposure
  • Smoking
  • Previous eye surgery
  • Congenital factors
  • Excessive ultraviolet exposure
📖 Study Notes

Manifestations

  • Painless progressive loss of vision
  • Blurred vision
  • Glare, especially at night
  • Difficulty reading
  • Reduced color perception
  • Frequent changes in spectacle prescription
📖 Study Notes

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is based on history and ophthalmic examination. Visual acuity testing and examination of the lens using appropriate equipment help determine the severity.

📖 Study Notes

Treatment

Definitive treatment for a visually significant cataract is surgical removal of the cloudy lens followed, when appropriate, by implantation of an intraocular lens.

📖 Study Notes

Nursing Management

  • Assess visual function and effect on daily activities.
  • Provide a safe environment to reduce falls.
  • Explain investigations and treatment.
  • Administer prescribed eye medications correctly.
  • Provide preoperative preparation.
  • Monitor after surgery for complications.
  • Teach correct postoperative eye care.
  • Advise the patient to avoid activities restricted by the surgeon.

📖 Study Notes

Definition

Glaucoma refers to a group of eye disorders characterized by progressive optic nerve damage, often associated with abnormal intraocular pressure.

📖 Study Notes

Types

  • Open-angle glaucoma
  • Angle-closure glaucoma
  • Secondary glaucoma
  • Congenital glaucoma
📖 Study Notes

Risk Factors

  • Increasing age
  • Family history
  • Diabetes
  • Previous eye injury
  • Certain medications
  • Vascular and ocular risk factors
📖 Study Notes

Manifestations

Chronic open-angle glaucoma may progress with few symptoms initially. Peripheral visual field loss may develop gradually.

Acute angle closure may present with severe eye pain, blurred vision, halos around lights, headache, nausea, vomiting and a red eye.

📖 Study Notes

Nursing Management

  • Administer prescribed antiglaucoma medications.
  • Monitor intraocular pressure as ordered.
  • Teach the patient correct eye-drop administration.
  • Stress adherence to treatment.
  • Educate about follow-up examinations.
  • Recognize acute angle closure as an ophthalmic emergency.

Definition

Conjunctivitis is inflammation of the conjunctiva. It may result from infectious or non-infectious causes.

📖 Study Notes

Causes

  • Bacterial infection
  • Viral infection
  • Allergic reaction
  • Chemical irritation
  • Foreign-body irritation
📖 Study Notes

Signs and Symptoms

  • Red eye
  • Eye discharge
  • Itching
  • Burning or irritation
  • Excessive tearing
  • Swollen eyelids
📖 Study Notes

Nursing Management

  • Maintain good hand hygiene.
  • Avoid sharing towels and personal items.
  • Administer prescribed medications.
  • Clean discharge appropriately.
  • Teach infection-control measures.
  • Encourage follow-up when symptoms persist or worsen.

📖 Study Notes

Retinal Detachment

Retinal detachment occurs when the retina separates from the underlying tissues that support its normal function. It requires urgent ophthalmic assessment.

Possible manifestations
  • Flashes of light
  • Floaters
  • Shadow or curtain across vision
  • Reduced vision
📖 Study Notes

Diabetic Retinopathy

Diabetic retinopathy results from diabetes-related damage to retinal blood vessels and may lead to visual impairment.

Nursing responsibilities
  • Promote glycemic control.
  • Encourage regular eye examinations.
  • Educate about warning symptoms.
  • Reinforce adherence to treatment.

📖 Study Notes

Preoperative Nursing Care

  • Confirm patient identity and procedure.
  • Review informed consent according to facility policy.
  • Assess baseline visual status.
  • Administer prescribed preoperative medications.
  • Explain postoperative expectations.
  • Prepare the patient physically and psychologically.
📖 Study Notes

Postoperative Care

  • Monitor pain and visual changes.
  • Administer prescribed eye drops.
  • Maintain eye protection as prescribed.
  • Observe for redness, discharge or excessive pain.
  • Teach the patient how to protect the eye.
  • Reinforce follow-up appointments.
📖 Study Notes

Possible Complications

  • Infection
  • Bleeding
  • Raised intraocular pressure
  • Wound problems
  • Visual deterioration
📖 Study Notes

Eye Drop Administration

  1. Perform hand hygiene.
  2. Explain the procedure.
  3. Position the patient appropriately.
  4. Pull the lower eyelid down gently.
  5. Instill the prescribed number of drops.
  6. Avoid touching the dropper tip to the eye.
  7. Ask the patient to close the eye gently.
  8. Perform hand hygiene again when appropriate.
📖 Study Notes

Patient Education

  • Use medications exactly as prescribed.
  • Do not share eye medications.
  • Attend scheduled follow-up appointments.
  • Report sudden visual loss or severe eye pain promptly.
  • Protect the eye from injury.
  • Maintain appropriate control of chronic conditions.

1. Discuss the nursing management of a patient with cataract.

Cataract causes progressive clouding of the lens and can interfere with a patient's ability to perform normal activities. Nursing care therefore focuses on assessment, safety, preparation for treatment, postoperative care and education.

Assessment: The nurse assesses visual acuity, degree of visual impairment, ability to perform activities of daily living, pain or discomfort, medical history and the patient's understanding of the condition.

Safety: Because reduced vision increases the risk of falls and injury, the environment should be well organized, adequately illuminated and free of unnecessary obstacles.

Preoperative care: The nurse prepares the patient for the planned procedure, administers prescribed medications, verifies required preparation and provides clear explanations.

Postoperative care: The nurse monitors visual changes, pain, redness, discharge and other possible complications. Prescribed medications are administered correctly.

Health education: The patient should understand medication use, eye protection, activity restrictions and the importance of follow-up appointments.

2. Discuss glaucoma under definition, types, manifestations, management and patient education.

Glaucoma is a group of disorders involving progressive optic nerve damage, often associated with abnormal intraocular pressure.

It includes open-angle and angle-closure forms as well as secondary and congenital forms.

Open-angle glaucoma may progress gradually and can initially produce few noticeable symptoms. Angle-closure glaucoma can present acutely with severe eye pain, blurred vision, halos, headache, nausea, vomiting and a red eye.

Management may include prescribed medications, laser procedures or surgery depending on the type and severity. Nursing care includes medication administration, monitoring, patient education and reinforcement of treatment adherence.

Patients should be taught to attend regular eye examinations, use medication correctly and seek urgent care for sudden severe eye pain or visual changes.

3. Discuss the nursing care of a patient undergoing ophthalmic surgery.

Nursing care begins before surgery with assessment, preparation, explanation of the procedure and administration of prescribed medications.

During the perioperative period, the nurse contributes to patient safety, infection prevention, correct positioning and adherence to the surgical plan.

After surgery, the nurse monitors the patient for pain, bleeding, infection, changes in vision and other complications. Prescribed medications and protective measures are maintained.

Before discharge, the nurse provides education concerning eye protection, medication administration, activity restrictions, warning signs and follow-up care.
