1. Surgical Asepsis

Meaning

Surgical asepsis refers to practices used to eliminate microorganisms and maintain a sterile field during procedures.

Important principles

  • Sterile objects should only contact sterile objects.
  • The sterile field should remain within view.
  • Moisture can contaminate a sterile field.
  • Sterile technique should be maintained throughout the procedure.
  • Any item of uncertain sterility should be considered contaminated.
2. Preoperative Nursing Care

Main goals

  • Assess the patient's physical condition.
  • Confirm patient identity and planned procedure.
  • Review allergies and relevant history.
  • Ensure required investigations are available.
  • Provide psychological preparation.
  • Confirm appropriate consent according to policy.
  • Prepare the patient according to the surgical procedure and local protocol.
3. Surgical Counts

What may be counted?

  • Sponges
  • Swabs
  • Needles
  • Instruments
  • Other countable items according to theatre policy

Counts help prevent retained surgical items and are performed according to institutional policy and the procedure being performed.

4. Patient Positioning

Proper positioning provides surgical access while helping to protect the patient from pressure injuries, nerve injury, respiratory compromise and other complications.

Examples

  • Supine position
  • Prone position
  • Lateral position
  • Lithotomy position
  • Trendelenburg position
  • Reverse Trendelenburg position
5. Postoperative Handover

The theatre nurse should provide a clear handover to the receiving team after surgery.

  • Patient identity and procedure performed
  • Relevant intraoperative events
  • Estimated blood loss where applicable
  • Specimens collected
  • Drains and dressings
  • Medications or treatments given
  • Important postoperative concerns
