1. Introduction to Nursing Research

Nursing research is the systematic investigation of questions relevant to nursing practice, education, administration and health outcomes.

  • Improves evidence-based nursing practice.
  • Identifies problems requiring investigation.
  • Evaluates nursing interventions.
  • Improves quality and safety of patient care.
  • Contributes to development of nursing knowledge.
2. Research Problem and Problem Statement

A research problem is an issue, gap or concern that requires systematic investigation.

A good problem statement should:

  • Clearly identify the problem.
  • Provide relevant context.
  • Indicate the population or setting where appropriate.
  • Explain why the problem is important.
  • Be supported by available evidence.
3. Research Objectives and Questions

Research objectives describe what the researcher intends to accomplish.

Objectives should generally be:

  • Specific
  • Measurable
  • Achievable
  • Relevant
  • Time-bound where appropriate

Research questions translate the research problem into questions that the study will investigate.

4. Literature Review

A literature review is a critical examination and synthesis of existing knowledge related to the research problem.

  • Identifies what is already known.
  • Identifies gaps in knowledge.
  • Provides background for the study.
  • Helps refine research questions.
  • Supports selection of appropriate methodology.
  • Helps avoid unnecessary duplication of research.
5. Theoretical and Conceptual Frameworks

A theoretical framework uses an established theory to guide the study.

A conceptual framework illustrates how important concepts or variables are expected to relate to one another within the study.

  • Provides structure for the study.
  • Guides variable selection.
  • Helps explain relationships among concepts.
  • Supports interpretation of findings.
6. Research Designs

Common research designs include:

  • Descriptive designs
  • Cross-sectional studies
  • Correlational studies
  • Experimental studies
  • Quasi-experimental studies
  • Case study designs
  • Qualitative designs
  • Mixed-methods designs

The choice of design depends on the research question, objectives, available resources and ethical considerations.

7. Population, Sample and Sampling

The target population is the broader group to which the researcher intends the study to apply.

A sample is a subset of the population selected for participation.

Sampling methods

  • Simple random sampling
  • Systematic sampling
  • Stratified sampling
  • Cluster sampling
  • Convenience sampling
  • Purposive sampling
  • Snowball sampling
8. Variables and Operational Definitions

A variable is a characteristic or attribute that can take different values.

  • Independent variable: The variable considered as a potential cause or influence.
  • Dependent variable: The outcome being measured.
  • Confounding variable: A variable that may distort the relationship between variables.

An operational definition explains exactly how a concept or variable will be measured in a particular study.

9. Data Collection Methods and Tools

Common methods include:

  • Questionnaires
  • Interviews
  • Observation
  • Record review
  • Focus group discussions
  • Measurements and clinical assessments

A data collection tool should be appropriate for the research objectives and population.

10. Validity and Reliability

Validity refers to how well a tool measures what it is intended to measure.

Reliability refers to the consistency or stability of measurement.

A tool may be reliable without being valid, so both concepts must be considered during research planning.

11. Research Ethics

Important ethical principles include:

  • Respect for persons
  • Informed consent
  • Confidentiality
  • Privacy
  • Beneficence
  • Non-maleficence
  • Justice
  • Voluntary participation
  • Right to withdraw

Research involving human participants requires appropriate ethical review and approval according to applicable institutional and regulatory requirements.

12. Data Analysis

Data analysis involves organizing, examining and interpreting collected information.

Quantitative analysis may include:

  • Frequencies
  • Percentages
  • Means
  • Standard deviations
  • Measures of association
  • Appropriate statistical tests

Qualitative analysis may involve:

  • Coding
  • Categorization
  • Theme development
  • Interpretation
13. Research Proposal Structure

A research proposal commonly contains:

  • Title
  • Introduction/background
  • Problem statement
  • Justification/significance
  • Objectives
  • Research questions or hypotheses where appropriate
  • Literature review
  • Theoretical/conceptual framework
  • Methodology
  • Ethical considerations
  • Work plan
  • Budget
  • References
  • Appendices
14. Case Study Development

A case study is an in-depth investigation of an individual, family, group, organization or situation within its real-life context.

A nursing case study may include:

  • Patient identification information
  • Presenting complaint
  • History of present illness
  • Past medical and surgical history
  • Family and social history
  • Physical assessment
  • Investigations
  • Medical diagnosis
  • Nursing diagnoses
  • Nursing interventions
  • Evaluation
  • Discharge and follow-up planning
15. Research Report Writing and Presentation

A research report communicates the research process, findings and interpretation in a structured manner.

  • Present findings clearly.
  • Use appropriate tables and figures where necessary.
  • Interpret findings in relation to the research questions.
  • Discuss limitations.
  • Draw conclusions supported by the findings.
  • Make recommendations based on the evidence.
  • Use appropriate referencing.

Quantitative vs Qualitative Research

Quantitative research generally focuses on numerical data, measurement and statistical analysis.

Qualitative research generally explores meanings, experiences, perceptions and processes using non-numerical data.

Validity vs Reliability

Validity: Whether the research tool measures what it is intended to measure.

Reliability: Whether the measurement is consistent or stable.

Research Ethics Quick Review
  • Obtain informed consent.
  • Protect confidentiality.
  • Respect privacy.
  • Minimize harm.
  • Promote potential benefits.
  • Ensure fair participant selection.
  • Respect the participant's right to withdraw.
Basic Research Proposal Formula

Problem → Objectives → Literature → Framework → Methodology → Ethics → Analysis → Reporting

Long-Answer Questions

Use these questions for examination practice.

1. Discuss the steps involved in developing a research proposal.

Development of a research proposal begins with identification of a research problem. The researcher investigates the problem and determines why it requires study.

The researcher then develops objectives and research questions or hypotheses where appropriate.

A literature review is conducted to identify existing knowledge, gaps and relevant theoretical or conceptual perspectives.

The methodology is then developed. It includes the research design, study population, sample, sampling procedure, data collection methods, instruments and data analysis plan.

Ethical considerations, work plans, budgets, references and appendices are also incorporated according to institutional requirements.

2. Discuss the importance of research ethics in nursing research.

Research ethics protect the rights, dignity, safety and welfare of research participants.

Participants should receive adequate information and provide voluntary informed consent before participating.

Researchers should protect privacy and confidentiality and minimize potential harm.

Ethical principles such as beneficence, non-maleficence, justice and respect for persons guide responsible research.

Appropriate ethical review and approval are required according to relevant institutional and regulatory requirements.

3. Discuss the importance of literature review in research.

Literature review helps the researcher understand existing knowledge about the research problem.

It identifies knowledge gaps, provides background information and helps establish the significance of the proposed study.

It can also assist in refining research questions, selecting an appropriate methodology and identifying relevant theories or concepts.

A good literature review critically compares and synthesizes relevant sources rather than simply listing them.

4. Discuss the components of a nursing case study.

A nursing case study generally begins with relevant patient identification information while maintaining confidentiality.

It includes the presenting complaint, history of the present illness, past medical and surgical history, family history and relevant social history.

A comprehensive physical assessment and relevant investigations are documented.

The case study then discusses the medical diagnosis and nursing diagnoses, followed by nursing interventions and evaluation.

Patient education, discharge planning and follow-up may also be included.
