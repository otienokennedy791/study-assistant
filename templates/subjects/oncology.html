About Oncology Nursing

Oncology nursing focuses on the prevention, screening, diagnosis, treatment, supportive care and rehabilitation of patients with cancer. The oncology nurse also provides education, symptom management, psychosocial support and end-of-life care where appropriate.

Understanding cancer biology, risk factors, common manifestations, investigations, treatment modalities and nursing management is essential for safe and holistic care.

Study tip: Study each topic through its subtopics. Pay particular attention to cancer development, staging, treatment modalities, chemotherapy precautions, radiation care, pain management and patient education.
📖 Study Notes

Definition of Cancer

Cancer refers to a group of diseases characterized by abnormal cells that grow in an uncontrolled manner and may invade surrounding tissues or spread to distant sites.

📖 Study Notes

Characteristics of Cancer Cells

  • Uncontrolled proliferation
  • Reduced normal growth regulation
  • Ability to invade surrounding tissues
  • Potential to metastasize
  • Altered cellular differentiation
📖 Study Notes

Role of the Oncology Nurse

  • Patient assessment
  • Administration and monitoring of treatment
  • Symptom management
  • Patient and family education
  • Psychosocial support
  • Infection prevention
  • Coordination of care
  • Supportive and palliative care

Basic Cancer Development Concept

Cancer development involves abnormal cellular changes followed by uncontrolled growth and, in some cancers, invasion and metastasis.
📖 Study Notes

Risk Factors

  • Tobacco exposure
  • Excessive alcohol use
  • Some infectious agents
  • Radiation exposure
  • Occupational exposures
  • Some inherited genetic factors
  • Age
  • Dietary and lifestyle factors
📖 Study Notes

Primary Prevention

Primary prevention aims to reduce the occurrence of cancer before it develops.

  • Avoid tobacco products.
  • Reduce harmful alcohol exposure.
  • Maintain healthy lifestyle practices.
  • Use appropriate protection from occupational hazards.
  • Follow recommended vaccination programs.
📖 Study Notes

Secondary Prevention

Secondary prevention focuses on early detection through appropriate screening and prompt investigation of suspicious signs and symptoms.

📖 Study Notes

Classification

Cancers may be classified according to the tissue or cell type from which they originate.

  • Carcinomas
  • Sarcomas
  • Leukemias
  • Lymphomas
  • Myelomas
📖 Study Notes

Grading

Grading describes how abnormal cancer cells appear microscopically and provides information about their differentiation.

📖 Study Notes

Staging

Staging describes the extent of cancer in the body. A commonly used framework is TNM:

  • T: Primary tumour characteristics.
  • N: Regional lymph-node involvement.
  • M: Distant metastasis.

History and Physical Examination

Assessment begins with a detailed history and physical examination, including symptoms, risk factors, previous illness and family history where relevant.

📖 Study Notes

Diagnostic Investigations

  • Blood tests
  • Imaging studies
  • Endoscopy where indicated
  • Cytology
  • Biopsy
  • Histopathological examination
📖 Study Notes

Biopsy

Biopsy involves obtaining tissue for microscopic examination. Histopathological assessment can help establish the diagnosis and characteristics of a tumour.

📖 Study Notes

Surgery

Surgery may be used to remove a tumour, establish a diagnosis, relieve symptoms or provide reconstruction, depending on the clinical situation.

📖 Study Notes

Chemotherapy

Chemotherapy uses anticancer medicines to destroy or inhibit cancer cells. Some agents can also affect rapidly dividing normal cells.

📖 Study Notes

Radiotherapy

Radiotherapy uses ionizing radiation to damage cancer cells in a defined treatment area.

📖 Study Notes

Other Treatment Approaches

  • Hormonal therapy
  • Targeted therapy
  • Immunotherapy
  • Stem-cell or cellular therapies in selected conditions
  • Supportive and palliative treatment

📖 Study Notes

Pre-treatment Assessment

  • Assess general condition.
  • Review laboratory results as required.
  • Check relevant treatment history.
  • Assess allergies and previous reactions.
  • Explain the treatment plan and expected effects.
📖 Study Notes

Common Adverse Effects

  • Nausea and vomiting
  • Fatigue
  • Myelosuppression
  • Alopecia with some agents
  • Mucositis
  • Appetite changes
  • Organ-specific toxicities depending on the drug
📖 Study Notes

Myelosuppression

Suppression of bone-marrow activity may reduce blood-cell production and increase the risk of infection, anemia and bleeding.

Nursing care
  • Monitor relevant blood counts.
  • Observe for signs of infection.
  • Monitor for bleeding.
  • Use appropriate infection-prevention measures.
  • Teach the patient when to report warning symptoms.

📖 Study Notes

External Beam Radiation

External beam radiotherapy directs radiation from a machine toward the treatment area.

📖 Study Notes

Skin Care

  • Inspect the treatment area.
  • Teach gentle skin care.
  • Avoid unnecessary friction or irritation.
  • Follow the treatment team's instructions regarding topical products.
  • Report significant skin reactions.
📖 Study Notes

Fatigue and Nutrition

Fatigue is common during cancer treatment. Nurses should assess fatigue, encourage appropriate activity and rest, and support adequate nutrition and hydration according to the patient's condition.

📖 Study Notes

Pain Assessment

Pain should be assessed systematically, including location, intensity, quality, duration, aggravating factors and effect on function.

📖 Study Notes

Pharmacological Management

Analgesic treatment is selected according to the patient's pain and clinical needs. Prescribed medicines should be administered safely and their effectiveness monitored.

📖 Study Notes

Non-pharmacological Measures

  • Positioning
  • Relaxation techniques
  • Appropriate distraction
  • Psychological support
  • Comfort measures
  • Family support
📖 Study Notes

Psychosocial Support

A cancer diagnosis may affect emotions, relationships, finances, employment and the patient's sense of identity. The nurse should provide therapeutic communication, emotional support and appropriate referral.

📖 Study Notes

Palliative Care

Palliative care focuses on improving quality of life by addressing physical, psychological, social and spiritual needs of patients with serious illness.

📖 Study Notes

Family Support

  • Provide clear information.
  • Encourage appropriate communication.
  • Assess caregiver needs.
  • Provide emotional support.
  • Link families with appropriate services.
📖 Study Notes

Oncology Emergencies

Some cancer-related complications can become urgent or life-threatening and require rapid recognition.

  • Febrile neutropenia
  • Spinal cord compression
  • Superior vena cava syndrome
  • Tumour lysis syndrome
  • Severe hypercalcemia
📖 Study Notes

Patient Education

  • Medication adherence
  • Recognition of warning symptoms
  • Infection-prevention measures where indicated
  • Nutrition and hydration
  • Follow-up appointments
  • Safe management of treatment side effects

1. Discuss the nursing management of a patient receiving chemotherapy.

Nursing management begins with a comprehensive assessment of the patient's condition, treatment history, allergies and relevant laboratory findings. The nurse should also assess the patient's understanding of the treatment and provide appropriate education.

During administration, the nurse follows the prescribed chemotherapy protocol and institutional safety procedures. Appropriate precautions should be used when handling hazardous medicines.

The patient should be monitored for immediate reactions and treatment-related adverse effects. Common problems may include nausea, vomiting, fatigue, mucositis and myelosuppression.

The nurse should educate the patient about infection prevention, medication adherence, nutrition, hydration and symptoms that require prompt medical attention.

Psychosocial support is also important because cancer treatment can affect the patient's emotions, relationships, finances and daily functioning.

2. Discuss the nursing management of cancer pain.

Pain assessment should include the location, intensity, quality, duration and factors that aggravate or relieve pain. The effect of pain on sleep, mobility, appetite and emotional well-being should also be considered.

Prescribed analgesics should be administered safely and the patient's response should be reassessed. The nurse should monitor for therapeutic effects and adverse effects.

Non-pharmacological approaches such as positioning, relaxation, distraction and comfort measures may complement medication.

Persistent or poorly controlled pain should be communicated to the healthcare team so that the treatment plan can be reassessed.

3. Discuss the role of the nurse in palliative care.

Palliative nursing aims to improve quality of life by addressing physical, psychological, social and spiritual needs.

The nurse assesses symptoms such as pain, nausea, breathlessness, fatigue and anxiety and contributes to appropriate symptom management.

Therapeutic communication is essential. The nurse should listen to the patient and family, provide clear information and support informed participation in care decisions.

The nurse also supports caregivers, coordinates services and promotes dignity, comfort and appropriate continuity of care.
