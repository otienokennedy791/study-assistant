Oncology Nursing Study Guide

Oncology nursing involves the prevention, early detection, diagnosis, treatment and supportive care of patients with cancer. It requires knowledge of cancer biology, treatment modalities, symptom management, psychosocial care, palliative care and patient education.

Study approach: Open each topic and work through every subtopic. Mark completed subtopics in the Progress section as you study.
1. Introduction to Oncology and Cancer

1.1 Meaning of Oncology

Oncology is the branch of medicine and nursing concerned with the study, diagnosis, treatment and care of people affected by cancer.

An oncology nurse provides physical, psychological, social and educational support throughout the cancer care continuum.

1.2 Definition of Cancer

Cancer is a group of diseases characterized by uncontrolled abnormal cell growth. Abnormal cells may invade surrounding tissues and can spread to distant organs through metastasis.

1.3 Benign and Malignant Tumours

Benign tumours

  • Usually grow slowly.
  • Often remain localized.
  • Usually have well-defined borders.
  • Do not normally metastasize.

Malignant tumours

  • Can invade surrounding tissues.
  • May grow rapidly.
  • Can metastasize.
  • May cause systemic effects.

1.4 Cancer Terminology

  • Neoplasm: abnormal new growth of tissue.
  • Tumour: abnormal mass resulting from excessive cell growth.
  • Metastasis: spread of malignant cells to distant sites.
  • Carcinoma: cancer arising from epithelial tissue.
  • Sarcoma: cancer arising from connective or supporting tissues.
  • Leukaemia: malignant disorder involving blood-forming tissues.
  • Lymphoma: malignancy involving lymphoid tissues.

2. Cancer Risk Factors and Prevention

2.1 Modifiable Risk Factors

  • Tobacco use.
  • Excessive alcohol consumption.
  • Unhealthy diet.
  • Physical inactivity.
  • Obesity.
  • Excessive exposure to ultraviolet radiation.
  • Some occupational exposures.

2.2 Non-Modifiable Risk Factors

  • Age.
  • Genetic predisposition.
  • Family history.
  • Previous cancer history.
  • Certain inherited genetic mutations.

2.3 Infectious Causes

Some infections are associated with increased cancer risk. Examples include human papillomavirus, hepatitis B virus, hepatitis C virus, Epstein-Barr virus and certain Helicobacter pylori infections.

2.4 Primary Prevention

  • Smoking cessation.
  • Healthy nutrition.
  • Regular physical activity.
  • Maintaining a healthy body weight.
  • Vaccination where appropriate.
  • Reducing harmful occupational exposures.
  • Sun protection.

2.5 Secondary Prevention

Secondary prevention involves early detection through appropriate screening and prompt evaluation of suspicious findings.

3. Cancer Biology and Pathophysiology

3.1 Normal Cell Growth

Normal cells grow, divide and die in a controlled manner. Cell growth is regulated by genetic and biochemical signals.

3.2 Abnormal Cell Growth

Cancer develops when genetic changes interfere with normal mechanisms controlling cell growth, division and cell death.

3.3 Hallmarks of Malignancy

  • Sustained proliferative signalling.
  • Resistance to cell death.
  • Ability to invade surrounding tissues.
  • Ability to stimulate blood vessel formation.
  • Ability to evade immune destruction.
  • Ability to metastasize.

3.4 Metastasis

Metastasis occurs when malignant cells leave the primary tumour, enter lymphatic or blood vessels, travel to another location, exit the circulation and establish a secondary tumour.

4. Assessment and Diagnosis of Cancer

4.1 Health History

The nurse assesses presenting symptoms, duration, severity, previous illnesses, family history, lifestyle factors, occupational exposures and previous cancer screening.

4.2 Physical Examination

Physical assessment may include general appearance, nutritional status, lymph nodes, skin, respiratory system, abdomen, neurological status and examination of the affected organ.

4.3 Diagnostic Investigations

  • Blood tests.
  • Imaging studies.
  • Endoscopy.
  • Biopsy.
  • Cytology.
  • Molecular and genetic tests where indicated.

4.4 Biopsy

A biopsy involves obtaining tissue or cells for pathological examination. Histological examination can help determine whether malignancy is present and identify tumour characteristics.

4.5 Staging

Staging describes the extent of cancer in the body. The TNM system is commonly used for many solid tumours:

  • T: characteristics and extent of the primary tumour.
  • N: involvement of regional lymph nodes.
  • M: presence or absence of distant metastasis.

5. Cancer Treatment Modalities

5.1 Surgery

Surgery may be used to diagnose, stage, remove or control a tumour. Nursing care includes preoperative preparation, postoperative monitoring, pain management and prevention of complications.

5.2 Chemotherapy

Chemotherapy uses medicines that interfere with cancer cell growth and division. Some chemotherapy drugs also affect rapidly dividing normal cells, resulting in adverse effects.

Common adverse effects

  • Nausea and vomiting.
  • Fatigue.
  • Myelosuppression.
  • Alopecia.
  • Mucositis.
  • Peripheral neuropathy with some agents.

5.3 Radiation Therapy

Radiation therapy uses ionizing radiation to damage cancer cells. It may be delivered externally or internally depending on the treatment plan.

Possible effects

  • Skin reactions.
  • Fatigue.
  • Site-specific tissue effects.

5.4 Immunotherapy

Immunotherapy helps the immune system recognize or attack cancer cells. Adverse effects vary according to the agent and may involve immune-mediated inflammation.

5.5 Targeted Therapy

Targeted therapies act on specific molecular features involved in cancer growth and survival.

5.6 Hormonal Therapy

Hormonal therapy may be used for cancers whose growth is influenced by hormones, such as some breast and prostate cancers.

6. Nursing Management of the Patient with Cancer

6.1 Nursing Assessment

  • Assess pain and other symptoms.
  • Assess nutritional and hydration status.
  • Monitor vital signs.
  • Assess functional ability.
  • Assess treatment-related adverse effects.
  • Assess emotional and psychological needs.
  • Assess family and social support.

6.2 Pain Management

Cancer pain should be assessed systematically. Management may involve pharmacological and non-pharmacological interventions.

  • Assess location, intensity, quality and timing of pain.
  • Administer prescribed analgesics.
  • Monitor response and adverse effects.
  • Use relaxation and other supportive techniques.

6.3 Nutritional Management

  • Monitor weight and nutritional intake.
  • Provide small frequent meals where appropriate.
  • Manage nausea and vomiting.
  • Provide oral care for mucositis.
  • Refer to a dietitian when appropriate.

6.4 Infection Prevention

Patients receiving treatments that suppress bone marrow may have increased infection risk. Nurses should monitor for fever and other signs of infection and follow appropriate infection-prevention measures.

6.5 Psychosocial Support

Cancer may cause fear, anxiety, depression, altered body image, financial stress and changes in family roles. Therapeutic communication and appropriate referral are important.

7. Common Oncology Emergencies

7.1 Febrile Neutropenia

Febrile neutropenia is a potentially serious complication in patients receiving myelosuppressive cancer treatment. Fever in a patient with significant neutropenia requires urgent clinical assessment.

7.2 Tumour Lysis Syndrome

Tumour lysis syndrome can occur when large numbers of tumour cells break down rapidly, releasing intracellular contents into the bloodstream.

Possible abnormalities include:

  • Hyperkalaemia.
  • Hyperphosphataemia.
  • Hypocalcaemia.
  • Hyperuricaemia.

7.3 Superior Vena Cava Syndrome

Compression or obstruction of the superior vena cava can impair venous drainage from the head, neck and upper limbs.

7.4 Spinal Cord Compression

Malignant spinal cord compression may cause back pain, weakness, sensory changes and bladder or bowel dysfunction. It requires urgent assessment.

8. Palliative Care and End-of-Life Nursing

8.1 Meaning of Palliative Care

Palliative care focuses on improving quality of life by preventing and relieving suffering and addressing physical, psychological, social and spiritual needs.

8.2 Symptom Management

  • Pain.
  • Breathlessness.
  • Nausea and vomiting.
  • Constipation.
  • Fatigue.
  • Anxiety and distress.

8.3 Communication

Nurses should communicate honestly, compassionately and respectfully while considering the patient's preferences, understanding and cultural context.

8.4 Family Support

Families may need education, emotional support and assistance with caregiving responsibilities. Bereavement support may also be necessary.

8.5 End-of-Life Care

End-of-life nursing focuses on comfort, dignity, symptom control, respect for patient wishes and support for family members.

Diagram: Holistic Palliative Care
9. Patient Education and Survivorship

9.1 Treatment Education

Patients should understand the purpose of treatment, expected effects, possible adverse effects and when to seek urgent medical attention.

9.2 Medication Education

  • Explain medication purpose.
  • Explain administration instructions.
  • Explain common adverse effects.
  • Explain danger signs requiring medical review.
  • Encourage adherence to prescribed treatment.

9.3 Survivorship

Survivorship care may include monitoring for recurrence, managing long-term effects of treatment, rehabilitation, psychosocial support and health promotion.

9.4 Lifestyle Support

  • Healthy nutrition.
  • Appropriate physical activity.
  • Avoidance of tobacco.
  • Moderation or avoidance of alcohol according to clinical advice.
  • Routine follow-up and screening.
10. Oncology Nursing Ethics and Professional Practice

10.1 Autonomy

Patients have the right to participate in decisions about their own care after receiving appropriate information.

10.2 Informed Consent

Nurses support the informed-consent process by ensuring that patients receive appropriate information, understand what is being proposed and have opportunities to ask questions.

10.3 Confidentiality

Patient information should be protected and disclosed only in accordance with professional, legal and institutional requirements.

10.4 Ethical Decision-Making

Oncology nurses may encounter ethical issues involving treatment choices, disclosure of information, end-of-life decisions, resource allocation and research participation.

10.5 Professional Responsibilities

  • Advocate for patients.
  • Maintain professional competence.
  • Communicate effectively with the multidisciplinary team.
  • Maintain accurate documentation.
  • Respect patient dignity and rights.
  • Provide evidence-informed care.

Short-Answer Questions

Use these questions to test recall and understanding.

1. Define cancer.

Cancer is a group of diseases characterized by uncontrolled abnormal cell growth, with the potential to invade surrounding tissues and metastasize.

2. State five risk factors for cancer.

Tobacco use, excessive alcohol consumption, obesity, physical inactivity and certain infectious or occupational exposures.

3. What is metastasis?

Metastasis is the spread of malignant cancer cells from a primary site to distant organs or tissues.

4. State four common adverse effects of chemotherapy.

Nausea and vomiting, myelosuppression, fatigue and alopecia.

5. Mention four oncology emergencies.

Febrile neutropenia, tumour lysis syndrome, superior vena cava syndrome and spinal cord compression.

6. State four components of holistic palliative care.

Physical, psychological, social and spiritual care.

Long-Answer Questions

These questions are designed for examination-style discussion. Give definitions, explanations, examples and nursing applications.

1. Discuss the nursing management of a patient receiving chemotherapy.

Answer points:
  1. Assess the patient's baseline health status and treatment history.
  2. Monitor vital signs and general condition.
  3. Assess for nausea, vomiting, pain, fatigue and other adverse effects.
  4. Monitor for signs of myelosuppression and infection.
  5. Provide appropriate nutritional and hydration support.
  6. Provide oral care to reduce complications of mucositis.
  7. Administer prescribed supportive medications.
  8. Educate the patient about treatment and danger signs.
  9. Provide psychological support.
  10. Document treatment response and communicate significant findings.

2. Discuss the role of the nurse in palliative care.

Answer points:
  1. Assess physical symptoms such as pain and breathlessness.
  2. Provide or coordinate symptom management.
  3. Provide psychological and emotional support.
  4. Support communication between patients, families and healthcare providers.
  5. Respect patient preferences and dignity.
  6. Support family caregivers.
  7. Provide health education.
  8. Coordinate multidisciplinary care.
  9. Support the patient and family during end-of-life care.
  10. Provide or facilitate bereavement support where appropriate.

3. Discuss the prevention and early detection of cancer.

Answer points:
  1. Promote avoidance of tobacco.
  2. Promote healthy nutrition and physical activity.
  3. Encourage maintenance of a healthy body weight.
  4. Promote appropriate vaccination.
  5. Reduce harmful occupational and environmental exposures.
  6. Promote appropriate cancer screening.
  7. Teach patients to report persistent or unusual symptoms.
  8. Encourage timely medical assessment of suspicious findings.
  9. Provide community health education.
