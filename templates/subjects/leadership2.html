About This Course

NURS 378 develops advanced knowledge and practical skills in nursing leadership and management. The course focuses on managing people, resources and services while maintaining safe, effective and patient-centred nursing care.

Each topic below is divided into detailed subtopics so that you can study one area at a time. Relevant clinical examples, diagrams, short-answer questions and long-answer questions are included.

1. Human Resource Management in Nursing

▼

1.1 Meaning of Human Resource Management

Human resource management is the systematic process of planning, recruiting, selecting, developing, motivating, retaining and evaluating employees so that an organization can achieve its goals.

In nursing, human resource management ensures that the right number of appropriately qualified nurses are available in the right areas at the right time.

1.2 Importance in Nursing Services

  • Ensures adequate staffing.
  • Improves quality and continuity of patient care.
  • Promotes staff development.
  • Supports employee motivation and retention.
  • Reduces unnecessary workload.
  • Promotes safe working conditions.

1.3 Recruitment and Selection

Recruitment involves attracting qualified candidates, while selection involves choosing the candidate who best meets the requirements of the position.

Nursing managers should consider qualifications, registration status, experience, communication skills, professional behaviour and the needs of the institution.

1.4 Staff Retention

Retention refers to efforts aimed at keeping competent employees within an organization.

  • Fair workload.
  • Supportive leadership.
  • Professional development.
  • Recognition of good performance.
  • Safe working environment.
  • Opportunities for career progression.

2. Staffing and Scheduling

▼

2.1 Meaning of Staffing

Staffing is the process of determining the number and categories of personnel required and assigning them to appropriate positions and shifts.

2.2 Factors Affecting Staffing

  • Number of patients.
  • Patient acuity and dependency.
  • Available nursing staff.
  • Skill mix.
  • Ward layout and workload.
  • Working hours.
  • Staff leave and absenteeism.
  • Hospital policies and regulations.

2.3 Staff Scheduling

Scheduling involves preparing duty rosters that allocate staff to shifts while considering patient needs, staff availability, fairness and service requirements.

2.4 Principles of Effective Scheduling

  • Ensure adequate coverage.
  • Maintain appropriate skill mix.
  • Distribute shifts fairly.
  • Consider staff leave.
  • Avoid excessive consecutive shifts.
  • Consider continuity of care.
  • Follow institutional policies.

7. Change Management in Nursing

▼

7.1 Meaning of Change Management

Change management is the systematic process of preparing, supporting and guiding individuals and organizations through changes in policies, practices, technology or organizational systems.

7.2 Causes of Change

  • New technology.
  • Changes in healthcare policies.
  • Evidence-based practice.
  • Patient safety requirements.
  • Changes in staffing.
  • Financial pressures.
  • Changing patient needs.

7.3 Resistance to Change

Resistance occurs when individuals or groups oppose or hesitate to accept a proposed change.

Common causes include fear of the unknown, inadequate communication, previous negative experiences, lack of involvement and concern about increased workload.

7.4 Strategies for Managing Change

  • Explain the reason for change.
  • Involve staff in planning.
  • Provide adequate training.
  • Listen to concerns.
  • Provide resources.
  • Introduce change in manageable stages.
  • Monitor and evaluate results.

9. Risk Management and Patient Safety

▼

9.1 Meaning of Risk Management

Risk management is the systematic identification, assessment, control and monitoring of risks that may harm patients, staff or the organization.

9.2 Common Risks in Nursing Practice

  • Medication errors.
  • Patient falls.
  • Healthcare-associated infections.
  • Pressure injuries.
  • Communication failures.
  • Incorrect patient identification.
  • Equipment-related incidents.

9.3 Risk Assessment

Risk assessment involves identifying hazards, determining the likelihood and consequences of harm, and deciding appropriate control measures.

9.4 Incident Reporting

Incident reporting provides a structured way of documenting adverse events, near misses and other safety concerns. Reporting should support learning and improvement rather than simply assigning blame.

10. Strategic Nursing Leadership and Professional Practice

▼

10.1 Strategic Leadership

Strategic leadership involves guiding an organization toward long-term goals while considering internal capabilities, external changes, available resources and patient needs.

10.2 Vision and Mission

A mission describes the fundamental purpose of an organization, while a vision describes the desired future state.

Nursing leaders use these concepts to guide planning, decision making and service improvement.

10.3 Professional Accountability

Professional accountability means accepting responsibility for one's actions, decisions and professional standards.

  • Practising within scope of practice.
  • Maintaining competence.
  • Protecting patient confidentiality.
  • Documenting accurately.
  • Delegating appropriately.
  • Reporting unsafe practice.

10.4 Ethical Leadership

Ethical leadership requires leaders to make decisions that respect patients, staff, professional standards, fairness, confidentiality and human dignity.

10.5 Advocacy

Nurse leaders advocate for patients, staff and improved healthcare services. Advocacy may involve communicating patient needs, supporting safe staffing, improving resources and promoting patient rights.

1. Discuss the role of the nurse manager in human resource management.

Answer:

The nurse manager plays an important role in ensuring that adequate and competent nursing personnel are available to provide safe patient care. This begins with assessing the staffing requirements of the unit according to patient numbers, patient acuity, workload and skill mix.

The nurse manager participates in recruitment and selection, orientation of new staff and allocation of personnel to appropriate duties. The manager must ensure that staff members understand their responsibilities and work within their professional scope.

Another important responsibility is staff development. Managers identify knowledge and skill gaps through supervision and performance appraisal and then arrange appropriate education, mentorship, coaching or continuing professional development.

The nurse manager also promotes staff motivation and retention by maintaining effective communication, recognizing good performance, addressing workplace concerns and promoting a supportive working environment.

Finally, the manager monitors staff performance, manages absenteeism, participates in workforce planning and evaluates whether staffing decisions are improving patient outcomes and quality of care.

2. Discuss the principles of effective staffing and scheduling in a nursing unit.

Answer:

Effective staffing begins with assessment of patient needs. The nurse manager should consider the number of patients, their level of dependency, acuity and the amount of nursing care required.

The manager should then determine the appropriate number and categories of nursing personnel required. Skill mix is important because different patients require different levels of professional knowledge and technical skills.

Duty rosters should distribute shifts fairly and consider staff availability, leave, absenteeism and organizational requirements. Excessive consecutive shifts should be avoided where possible because fatigue can affect performance and patient safety.

Effective scheduling should also support continuity of care. Managers should review the roster regularly and make changes when patient numbers or acuity change.

Finally, staffing effectiveness should be evaluated using indicators such as workload, patient outcomes, staff satisfaction, overtime and incidents.

3. Explain how a nurse manager can successfully implement change in a healthcare organization.

Answer:

Successful change begins by identifying a clear need for change. The nurse manager should determine what problem is occurring and gather relevant information to demonstrate why the change is necessary.

Staff should then be involved in planning. Involvement allows employees to express concerns, contribute ideas and develop ownership of the proposed change.

Communication is essential throughout the process. The manager should clearly explain what will change, why it is necessary, how it will affect staff and what support will be provided.

Appropriate training and resources should be provided before implementation. If possible, the change can be introduced gradually so that problems can be identified and corrected.

After implementation, the nurse manager should evaluate outcomes. Feedback from staff and patients, quality indicators and safety data can be used to determine whether the change achieved its intended objectives.

4. Discuss the role of the nurse leader in promoting patient safety and risk management.

Answer:

The nurse leader promotes patient safety by creating an environment where risks are identified early and addressed systematically. This requires regular assessment of clinical processes and the environment.

The leader should ensure that staff follow appropriate procedures for medication administration, infection prevention, patient identification, documentation and other safety-sensitive activities.

Education and supervision are also important. Staff should receive appropriate training and should be supported when they require additional knowledge or skills.

Incident and near-miss reporting should be encouraged. Information obtained from incidents can be used to identify system weaknesses and develop corrective measures.

The nurse leader should also monitor patient safety indicators, implement quality improvement strategies and involve the multidisciplinary team in reducing preventable harm.

5. Explain the importance of financial and material resource management in nursing.

Answer:

Financial and material resource management ensures that healthcare organizations have the resources necessary to provide safe and effective patient care.

Proper budgeting helps nurse managers identify priorities, plan expenditure and control unnecessary spending. It also promotes accountability because managers can compare planned expenditure with actual expenditure.

Material management ensures that medicines, equipment and supplies are available when required. Proper stock control reduces shortages, wastage and expiry.

Nurse managers also have a responsibility to ensure that equipment is used correctly, maintained appropriately and replaced when necessary.

Effective resource management therefore supports both financial sustainability and quality patient care.
