About This Course

Curriculum development is a systematic process of identifying educational needs, formulating objectives, selecting and organizing learning experiences, implementing instruction and evaluating educational outcomes.

In nursing education, curriculum development connects theoretical knowledge with clinical competence, professional values and the healthcare needs of the population.

1. Introduction to Curriculum Development

▼

1.1 Meaning of Curriculum

A curriculum is a structured plan of learning that specifies what learners should know, understand, value and be able to do as a result of an educational programme.

1.2 Curriculum Development

Curriculum development is the systematic process of planning, designing, implementing and evaluating a curriculum.

1.3 Importance of Curriculum Development

  • Provides direction for teaching and learning.
  • Promotes consistency in education.
  • Links education with healthcare needs.
  • Defines expected learner competencies.
  • Guides assessment.
  • Supports quality improvement in education.

1.4 Curriculum Components

  • Educational philosophy.

2. Curriculum Philosophy and Foundations

▼

2.1 Meaning of Educational Philosophy

Educational philosophy refers to beliefs and principles about the purpose of education, the nature of learners, knowledge and the role of teachers.

2.2 Philosophical Foundations

  • Idealism.
  • Realism.
  • Pragmatism.
  • Existentialism.
  • Humanism.

2.3 Psychological Foundation

Psychology helps curriculum developers understand how learners develop, think, remember, become motivated and acquire skills.

2.4 Sociological Foundation

Society influences what should be taught. Nursing curricula should therefore respond to community health needs, cultural expectations, disease patterns and healthcare priorities.

2.5 Historical and Scientific Foundations

Historical developments in nursing and advances in healthcare science influence the knowledge, skills and professional competencies included in curricula.

Nursing example: If healthcare increasingly depends on digital documentation, a nursing curriculum may need to include electronic health records and digital health competencies.

4.2 Characteristics of Good Objectives

Psychomotor

9. Assessment and Evaluation of Learning

▼

9.1 Meaning of Assessment

Assessment is the systematic collection of information about learner performance for the purpose of determining achievement and guiding learning.

9.2 Formative Assessment

Formative assessment takes place during learning and provides feedback that can be used to improve learner performance.

Examples include quizzes, classroom questions, assignments and feedback during clinical practice.

9.3 Summative Assessment

Summative assessment occurs at the end of a course, unit or programme to determine the extent to which learning objectives have been achieved.

9.4 Assessment Methods in Nursing

  • Written examinations.
  • Multiple-choice questions.
  • Short-answer questions.
  • Essays.
  • Objective structured clinical examinations.
  • Clinical observation.
  • Projects and portfolios.

9.5 Principles of Good Assessment

  • Validity.
  • Reliability.
  • Objectivity.
  • Fairness.
  • Practicality.
  • Transparency.

10. Define curriculum evaluation.

Answer: Curriculum evaluation is the systematic process of determining the effectiveness, value and relevance of a curriculum.

1. Discuss the major components of a curriculum.

Answer:

The first major component is the educational philosophy. Philosophy provides the beliefs and principles that guide the purpose of education and the way learners are viewed.

The second component is educational objectives or learning outcomes. These describe what learners should know, understand, value or be able to perform after completing the learning experience.

Content is another important component. It includes the knowledge, concepts, skills and attitudes selected for teaching. Content should be relevant, accurate, appropriate and related to the objectives.

Learning experiences and teaching methods determine how learners interact with the content. In nursing education, these may include lectures, discussions, demonstrations, simulation and clinical practice.

Finally, assessment and evaluation determine whether learners achieved the intended outcomes and whether the curriculum itself is effective. Evaluation findings can then be used to improve the curriculum.

2. Explain the importance of needs assessment in curriculum development.

Answer:

Needs assessment helps curriculum developers identify existing educational and healthcare problems. Without identifying actual needs, a curriculum may contain content that is irrelevant to learners or the population being served.

It helps determine the knowledge and skills required by graduates. For example, if healthcare facilities identify gaps in infection prevention practice, curriculum developers can ensure that infection prevention receives adequate attention.

Needs assessment also assists in setting priorities. Educational resources are often limited, so developers need to determine which needs are most important.

It also promotes stakeholder involvement. Students, teachers, healthcare professionals, employers and communities can provide information about current needs.

Therefore, needs assessment improves curriculum relevance, supports effective planning and helps produce graduates who can respond to actual healthcare needs.

3. Discuss the three domains of learning and their importance in nursing education.

Answer:

The cognitive domain relates to knowledge and intellectual abilities. It includes processes such as remembering, understanding, applying, analysing, evaluating and creating. In nursing, students use the cognitive domain when learning about disease processes, pharmacology and clinical decision making.

The affective domain relates to attitudes, values, beliefs, emotions and professional behaviour. Nursing students need appropriate attitudes toward patients, confidentiality, teamwork, dignity and professional responsibility.

The psychomotor domain involves physical and clinical skills. Examples include administering medication, performing wound care, taking vital signs and carrying out other nursing procedures.

The three domains are interconnected. A competent nurse requires knowledge, appropriate professional attitudes and practical skills. Therefore, a nursing curriculum should address all three domains rather than focusing only on theoretical knowledge.

4. Explain the process of curriculum evaluation and continuous improvement.

Answer:

Curriculum evaluation begins by determining what aspects of the curriculum need to be evaluated. These may include objectives, content, teaching methods, resources, assessment and learner outcomes.

Relevant information is then collected from sources such as students, teachers, examination results, clinical performance, graduates and employers.

The collected information is analysed to determine whether the curriculum is achieving its intended outcomes. Strengths, weaknesses and gaps are identified.

Curriculum developers then decide what changes are required. Content may be updated, teaching methods modified, assessment strategies improved or clinical experiences reorganized.

The revised curriculum is implemented and monitored again. This creates a continuous improvement cycle in which curriculum development responds to changes in healthcare, education and community needs.

5. Discuss the relationship between theoretical learning and clinical competence in nursing education.

Answer:

Theoretical learning provides the knowledge required for nurses to understand diseases, treatments, nursing principles, pharmacology, communication and professional responsibilities.

However, theoretical knowledge alone does not guarantee clinical competence. Students must have opportunities to apply what they have learned in realistic clinical situations.

Skills laboratories, demonstrations, simulation and clinical placements allow students to practise procedures and develop clinical judgement under appropriate supervision.

Assessment and feedback are also important because they help students identify areas requiring improvement and gradually develop confidence and competence.

Therefore, an effective nursing curriculum should deliberately connect classroom theory with practical learning so that graduates can integrate knowledge, skills and professional attitudes in patient care.
