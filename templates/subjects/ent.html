📚 ENT Topics

1. Anatomy and Physiology of the Ear

Main Parts

  • External ear
  • Middle ear
  • Inner ear

External Ear

Includes the pinna and external auditory canal. It collects sound waves and directs them toward the tympanic membrane.

Middle Ear

Contains the tympanic membrane and the three ossicles: malleus, incus and stapes.

Inner Ear

Contains the cochlea, vestibule and semicircular canals.

2. Hearing Assessment

Assessment History

  • Hearing difficulty
  • Ear pain
  • Ear discharge
  • Tinnitus
  • Vertigo
  • Noise exposure

Investigations

  • Whisper test
  • Rinne test
  • Weber test
  • Pure tone audiometry
  • Tympanometry
3. Otitis Externa

Otitis externa is inflammation or infection of the external auditory canal.

Risk Factors

  • Swimming
  • Excess moisture
  • Trauma from cotton buds
  • Skin disorders

Symptoms

  • Ear pain
  • Itching
  • Ear discharge
  • Swelling
  • Reduced hearing
4. Otitis Media

Otitis media is inflammation or infection of the middle ear.

Symptoms

  • Ear pain
  • Fever
  • Hearing loss
  • Irritability in children
  • Possible ear discharge

Complications

  • Mastoiditis
  • Tympanic membrane perforation
  • Hearing impairment
5. Hearing Loss

Types

  • Conductive hearing loss
  • Sensorineural hearing loss
  • Mixed hearing loss

Nursing Care

  • Face the patient while speaking.
  • Reduce background noise.
  • Speak clearly.
  • Provide appropriate communication assistance.
6. Tinnitus

Tinnitus is the perception of sound such as ringing or buzzing without an external sound source.

Possible Causes

  • Hearing loss
  • Noise exposure
  • Ear disease
  • Some medications
7. Ménière's Disease

Common Features

  • Vertigo
  • Fluctuating hearing loss
  • Tinnitus
  • Feeling of fullness in the ear

Nursing Care

  • Prevent falls.
  • Assist with mobility.
  • Maintain a safe environment.
  • Administer prescribed treatment.
8. Vertigo and Balance Disorders

Vertigo is the false sensation of movement, often described as spinning.

Nursing Priorities

  • Prevent falls.
  • Assist with ambulation.
  • Monitor nausea and vomiting.
  • Maintain a safe environment.
9. Anatomy and Physiology of the Nose and Sinuses

Functions of the Nose

  • Smell
  • Air filtration
  • Humidification
  • Warming inspired air

Paranasal Sinuses

  • Frontal
  • Maxillary
  • Ethmoid
  • Sphenoid
10. Epistaxis

Epistaxis means bleeding from the nose.

Basic First Aid

  • Keep the patient calm.
  • Sit upright.
  • Lean slightly forward.
  • Apply pressure to the soft part of the nose.
11. Sinusitis

Sinusitis is inflammation of the paranasal sinuses.

Symptoms

  • Nasal congestion
  • Facial pressure
  • Nasal discharge
  • Headache
  • Reduced sense of smell
12. Deviated Nasal Septum

A deviated nasal septum occurs when the nasal septum is displaced from the midline.

  • Nasal obstruction
  • Difficulty breathing through one side
  • Recurrent sinus problems
  • Snoring
13. Tonsillitis and Adenoid Disorders

Symptoms

  • Sore throat
  • Difficulty swallowing
  • Fever
  • Enlarged tonsils
  • Bad breath
  • Cervical lymphadenopathy

Airway compromise should be recognized promptly.

14. Laryngitis and Voice Disorders

Laryngitis is inflammation of the larynx and commonly causes hoarseness.

  • Hoarseness
  • Weak voice
  • Sore throat
  • Cough

Nursing Advice

  • Voice rest
  • Avoid smoking and irritants
  • Maintain appropriate hydration
15. Airway Emergencies and Tracheostomy Care

Airway Emergencies

Upper airway obstruction can become life-threatening and requires urgent assessment.

Tracheostomy Care

  • Assess airway patency.
  • Monitor respiratory status.
  • Maintain appropriate humidification.
  • Provide prescribed suctioning when indicated.
  • Assess the tracheostomy site.
  • Keep emergency equipment available according to local policy.

📝 ENT Revision Notes

Important ENT Nursing Principles
  • Assess airway and breathing first during emergencies.
  • Assess hearing and communication needs.
  • Prevent falls in patients with vertigo.
  • Maintain appropriate ear and nasal hygiene.
  • Recognize airway obstruction early.
  • Provide clear patient education.
Conductive vs Sensorineural Hearing Loss

Conductive Hearing Loss

Results from impaired transmission of sound through the external or middle ear.

Sensorineural Hearing Loss

Results from problems involving the cochlea, auditory nerve or related pathways.

Mixed Hearing Loss

Contains both conductive and sensorineural components.

Important ENT Emergency Signs
  • Severe airway obstruction
  • Stridor
  • Respiratory distress
  • Severe swelling
  • Uncontrolled bleeding
  • Altered consciousness

📖 Long-Answer Questions

1. Discuss the nursing management of a patient with otitis media.

Nursing management begins with assessment of ear pain, fever, hearing changes and discharge.

Assess vital signs and monitor for complications such as tympanic membrane perforation and mastoiditis.

Administer prescribed medications and analgesics, monitor response and provide appropriate patient education.

The patient should be advised to attend follow-up when symptoms persist or worsen.

2. Discuss the nursing management of a patient experiencing vertigo.

The priority is patient safety because vertigo increases the risk of falls.

Assist the patient with movement and maintain a safe environment.

Assess dizziness, nausea, vomiting, hearing changes and other associated symptoms.

Administer prescribed treatment and educate the patient about safety measures.

3. Discuss the nursing care of a patient with a tracheostomy.

The primary priority is maintaining airway patency and adequate ventilation.

Monitor respiratory rate, oxygenation, breath sounds and respiratory effort.

Assess the tracheostomy site and provide prescribed suctioning when indicated.

Maintain appropriate humidification and ensure emergency airway equipment is available according to local policy.

4. Discuss the causes, assessment and nursing management of epistaxis.

Causes include trauma, dry nasal mucosa, infection, bleeding disorders and medications affecting coagulation.

Assess the severity and duration of bleeding, vital signs and possible causes.

Basic first aid includes keeping the patient calm, sitting upright, leaning slightly forward and applying firm pressure to the soft part of the nose.

Severe, persistent or recurrent bleeding requires appropriate clinical assessment.
